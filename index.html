Chapter zero — The story

Lucía Marín grew up on the edge of Valencia, in a kitchen where lunch began at nine in the morning and never quite ended. Her grandmother Carmen cooked without scales or timers — a handful of rice, a pinch of saffron, “until it smells right.”

When Carmen turned ninety, Lucía began writing everything down: the paella that fed fourteen cousins, the gazpacho poured from a jug in August heat, the crema catalana burnt with a hot iron because nobody owned a torch.

Then she travelled — to Galician fish markets, Basque pintxo bars and Andalusian courtyards — collecting the dishes every Spanish family argues about.

Oro Rojo is that notebook, tested and translated for kitchens far from Spain.

Lucía Marín

Paella Valenciana

Serves 4 · 1 h 15 min

You’ll need

  • 400 g bomba rice
  • 600 g chicken thighs, in pieces
  • 300 g rabbit (or more chicken)
  • 200 g flat green beans
  • 150 g butter beans
  • 1 ripe tomato, grated
  • 1.2 l water or light stock
  • A good pinch of saffron
  • 1 tsp sweet pimentón
  • 80 ml olive oil
  • Rosemary, salt

How to

1

Heat the oil in a wide paella pan and brown the meat well on all sides, about 15 minutes.

2

Add the beans, cook 3 minutes, then stir in the tomato and pimentón.

3

Pour in the water, add saffron and salt, and simmer 20 minutes.

4

Scatter the rice evenly. Do not stir from now on. Cook 10 minutes on high, then 8 on low.

5

Turn the heat up for the last 2 minutes until it crackles. Lay rosemary on top, cover with a cloth, rest 5 minutes.

— page 74 —

Carmen’s note

Make it the night before. Gazpacho is better the next day — like most good gossip.

Oro RojoIV · De la Huerta

Gazpacho Andaluz

Serves 4 · 15 min + 2 h chilling

You’ll need

  • 1 kg very ripe tomatoes
  • 1 small cucumber, peeled
  • ½ green pepper
  • 1 garlic clove
  • 50 g day-old white bread
  • 80 ml extra virgin olive oil
  • 2 tbsp sherry vinegar
  • Salt, cold water

How to

1

Soak the bread in a little water for 5 minutes, then squeeze it out.

2

Blend the vegetables with the bread, garlic, vinegar and salt until very smooth.

3

With the blender running, pour in the olive oil in a thin stream.

4

Strain, thin with cold water if needed, and adjust salt and vinegar.

5

Chill at least 2 hours. Serve in glasses with a drizzle of oil.

— page 131 —

Carmen’s note

I burnt the sugar with a hot iron. A torch works too — it’s just less dramatic.

Oro RojoVI · Dulces

Crema Catalana

Serves 6 · 30 min + 4 h chilling

You’ll need

  • 1 l whole milk
  • 6 egg yolks
  • 150 g sugar, plus more for the top
  • 40 g cornstarch
  • Peel of 1 lemon
  • 1 cinnamon stick

How to

1

Warm the milk with lemon peel and cinnamon; leave 15 minutes to infuse.

2

Whisk the yolks, sugar and cornstarch until pale.

3

Strain the warm milk into the yolks while whisking, then return to the pan.

4

Cook on low, stirring constantly, until it coats a spoon. Do not boil.

5

Pour into shallow dishes, chill 4 hours, then sugar the top and caramelise.

— page 208 —

Sobremesa

the long talk at the table after the meal — and the questions people ask over coffee.

I’ve never cooked Spanish food. Is it for me?

Yes. Every recipe is written step by step, with timings and the key moments explained.

Can I find the ingredients outside Spain?

Almost always. Each recipe lists a swap for anything hard to find, like bomba rice or jamón.

Is there a vegetarian chapter?

De la Huerta is fully vegetarian, and many tapas are too — marked with a small leaf.

Metric or cups?

Both. Grams and millilitres first, cups and spoons alongside.
